\begin{abstract}
% Written after Section 5, per the ordering in paper/outline.md.
Retrieval-augmented generation is widely deployed on the premise that grounding a
language model in retrieved text suppresses hallucination, and the corresponding
fear is that degraded retrieval reintroduces it. Prior work measures how accuracy
decays as retrieval quality falls, but accuracy pools outcomes that call for
opposite engineering responses: a system that declines to answer, and a system that
answers fluently and wrongly. We measure the \emph{composition} of RAG failures
under controlled, per-item-exact retrieval corruption on \NUM{nQA} SQuAD~v2
questions, separating unsupported assertions from answers that are wrong but
faithful to the evidence retrieved. As corruption rises to 75\% of the context
window, accuracy falls by \NUM{deltaCorrectPartial} points while the rate of
unsupported assertion does not move at all: paired differences are bounded within
$\pm$\NUM{halluBoundPartial} points of zero. The lost answers become abstentions and
grounded errors, not fabrications. When the evidence is removed entirely, abstention
jumps to \NUM{abstainAtTotal}\% and unsupported assertion falls further. The
generator therefore distinguishes \emph{no} evidence from \emph{some} evidence, but
not good evidence from bad: its abstention behaviour is threshold-like rather than
graded, which is why partial corruption is the costly regime --- not because it
provokes fabrication, but because it goes undetected. A manipulation check against
naturally failed retrievals in the same corpus finds the synthetic perturbation
reproduces unaided retrieval failure on every outcome. Retrieval score carries some
pre-generation signal (AUROC \NUM{bestSilentAUROC} for unsupported assertion), but a
cost analysis shows a similarity gate is worth deploying only once a fabrication is
judged at least five times as costly as a correct answer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% WRITE FOURTH.
Retrieval-augmented generation \citep{lewis2020rag} is the standard industrial
answer to hallucination: retrieve evidence, condition on it, and the model stops
inventing. The premise holds only as far as retrieval does, and in deployment it
often does not --- knowledge bases contain near-duplicates, the answer-bearing
passage is frequently absent, and the top-$k$ neighbours of a query are routinely
topically adjacent but wrong.

What matters when retrieval fails is not only \emph{how much} accuracy falls but
\emph{what the system does instead}. A RAG system that responds ``I cannot answer
from the context'' has failed safely; one that produces a confident, fluent, wrong
answer from the same broken context has failed dangerously. Both register
identically as an accuracy drop. Existing RAG robustness benchmarks
\citep{chen2024rgb,cuconasu2024noise} report accuracy under noise, and
\citet{chen2024rgb} additionally scores \emph{negative rejection} as a capability,
but neither traces how the balance between abstention and assertion moves as
retrieval degrades continuously.

We ask two questions. First, as the fraction of a context window occupied by
related-but-wrong evidence rises from 0 to 1, how does the split between abstention
and unsupported assertion move? Second, can that failure be anticipated from
similarity scores alone --- signals that exist before the generator is invoked, and
so could gate it at zero additional model cost?

We find that the fear is misplaced, at least for the system studied here.
Degrading retrieval degrades accuracy substantially and does not increase
fabrication at all: the generator converts lost evidence into refusals and into
faithful reports of the wrong passage, while its rate of unsupported assertion
stays flat within measurement error. This is invisible to any analysis that scores
answers as merely right or wrong.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item Evidence that hallucination rate is invariant to retrieval degradation over a
      range that costs \NUM{deltaCorrectPartial} points of accuracy, with the null
      bounded at $\pm$\NUM{halluBoundPartial} points (Table~\ref{tab:noise}), and
      evidence that the generator's abstention is threshold-like rather than graded.
\item A failure taxonomy that separates unsupported assertion from grounded error,
      without which the above is unobservable, together with the finding that the
      derived silent-failure ratio is less informative than its components.
\item A perturbation protocol that fixes the corruption level of each individual
      context exactly, rather than poisoning a shared index, supporting per-item
      dose--response analysis (\S\ref{sec:method}); and a manipulation check
      against naturally occurring retrieval failures in the same corpus.
\item An evaluation of retrieval-side signals as a pre-generation abstention gate,
      with operating points (Table~\ref{tab:predict}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% WRITE THIRD — after the results exist and before the introduction.
\paragraph{RAG robustness benchmarks.}
\citet{chen2024rgb} introduce RGB and evaluate noise robustness, negative
rejection, information integration and counterfactual robustness; our four
perturbations follow that taxonomy and we claim no novelty for it.
\citet{cuconasu2024noise} report the counter-intuitive result that inserting
\emph{random} documents can raise accuracy while related, non-answer-bearing
documents depress it. Both report accuracy under a fixed noise setting; we vary the
corruption fraction continuously and report what the model does when it is wrong.

\paragraph{Position and distraction effects.}
\citet{liu2024lost} show accuracy depends on where the relevant passage sits in a
long context. Our position experiment (\S\ref{sec:results}) is an explicit
small-scale replication, not a new finding. \citet{shi2023distracted} document the
same sensitivity outside retrieval settings, and \citet{yoran2024robust} mitigate it
by training; we restrict ourselves to inference-time defences.

\paragraph{Abstention and confidence.}
\citet{kadavath2022know} elicit confidence from model internals and
\citet{asai2024selfrag} train a model to critique its own retrievals. Both require
model access or additional generation. We ask how far a retrieval score --- already
computed, free, and available before generation --- gets on its own, which sets the
baseline such methods must beat. Automated RAG evaluation frameworks
\citep{es2024ragas,saadfalcon2024ares} provide faithfulness metrics; our judge is
narrower by design, labelling only whether each claim is entailed by the context
actually shown to the generator.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% WRITE SECOND.
\paragraph{Data.}
\NUM{nQA} questions sampled from the SQuAD~v2 development set
\citep{rajpurkar2018squad2} with seed \NUM{randomSeed}: \NUM{nAnswerable}
answerable and \NUM{nUnanswerable} unanswerable. The unanswerable split is
essential rather than incidental --- the gold paragraph is present in the index and
topically perfect, yet contains no answer, which isolates abstention pressure from
retrieval quality. The corpus is every paragraph in the split, chunked at 256
tokens with 50-token overlap. Embeddings come from \NUM{embedModel} run locally on
CPU; retrieval is exact inner product over L2-normalised vectors (FAISS
\texttt{IndexFlatIP}), not an approximate index, so no ANN error is added to the
error under study. A local retriever makes the retrieval half of the pipeline
deterministic and reproducible without API access; it is also weaker than a
frontier embedding model, so absolute accuracy here is not comparable to SQuAD
leaderboard numbers, and more items fall into the evidence-absent regime the study
targets. The generator is \NUM{genModel} at temperature 0.

\paragraph{Perturbations.}
Corruption is applied to the context handed to the generator, not to the index.
Poisoning a shared index makes the noise level a property of the run: an individual
answer cannot be attributed to a known corruption level. Perturbing the context
fixes the fraction exactly per item and holds retrieval byte-identical across
conditions, so two conditions differ only in the named variable. We use four
perturbations --- \textsc{random} (irrelevant chunks), \textsc{related}
(lower-ranked real retrievals: adjacent, plausible, wrong), \textsc{counterfactual}
(the gold answer string in context replaced by a false one, following
\citet{longpre2021conflicts}), and \textsc{drop\_gold} (all gold chunks removed
while the question remains answerable) --- at fractions $\{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\}$
of a $K{=}4$ context. Each results row records how many slots were actually
perturbed, since \textsc{counterfactual} is a no-op when the gold string is absent
from the retrieved text.

\paragraph{Outcome taxonomy.}
Every generation is assigned exactly one label: \textsc{abstain} (declined),
\textsc{correct} (token F1 $\geq 0.5$ against gold), \textsc{unsupported} (asserts
something the retrieved context does not support), \textsc{incorrect\_grounded}
(wrong, but traceable to the context), or \textsc{invalid} (blocked or errored
generation, excluded from all rates). Only \textsc{unsupported} counts as
hallucination. Separating it from \textsc{incorrect\_grounded} is the point:
pooling them turns ``retrieval was bad, so the answer was bad'' into an apparent
finding about generation. Abstention is detected by rule; a refusal phrase followed
by a claim (``the context does not say, but it was 1241'') is scored as an
assertion, not an abstention.

\paragraph{Judging, and how little rests on it.}
For answerable items that were neither abstentions nor correct, a judge sees the
question, the answer, and \emph{the context that was actually shown to the
generator}, and labels each answer supported or unsupported. Unanswerable items
need no judge: SQuAD~v2 guarantees no answer exists, so any assertion is
unsupported by construction.

The consequence is worth stating precisely, because a study whose primary outcome
is assigned by a language model invites the obvious objection. Across
\NUM{nScoredRows} scored generations the judge is invoked on
\NUM{judgeInvocationPct}\% of items, and of the \NUM{nUnsupported} unsupported
labels that constitute the headline metric, \NUM{nUnsupportedRule} follow from the
rule and \NUM{nUnsupportedJudged} from the judge. The primary outcome is therefore
\NUM{ruleDerivedPct}\% rule-derived. It does not stand or fall on judge quality,
and a reader who distrusts LLM judgment entirely can discard the judged subset and
still recover the result.

Every judged row now stores the exact context the judge ruled on, so any verdict
can be re-audited or re-scored by a second model. Rows collected before this was
added cannot be: reconstructing their context would require replaying a stochastic
perturbation and assuming it landed identically. We report that limitation rather
than assert reproducibility we cannot demonstrate. We also
track \emph{parametric} correctness --- items answered correctly with the gold
document absent from context --- because such items inflate every clean-condition
baseline and are rarely separated out.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Rates carry 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals over questions. Comparisons between
conditions are paired bootstraps on the same questions, reported with two-sided
$p$. Where a condition is sampled more than once per item, samples are averaged
within an item before resampling, so repeated sampling cannot shrink an interval.

\paragraph{The manipulation works.}
Correctness falls monotonically with corruption and every step is significant under
a paired bootstrap on the same questions: $-3.3$, $-6.7$ and $-12.0$ points at
25\%, 50\% and 75\% respectively ($p<0.0001$ at 75\%). Whatever else the
perturbation does, it demonstrably degrades the evidence the generator receives.

\paragraph{Hallucination is invariant.}
Over the same range, the rate of unsupported assertion does not move. Paired
differences against the clean condition are $-0.007$, $+0.000$ and $+0.000$, with
$p=0.72$, $1.00$ and $1.00$ and intervals contained within $\pm0.027$. This is not
a weak trend but a tight null measured against a 12-point collapse in accuracy.

Table~\ref{tab:noise} shows where the lost answers go. Of 18 correct answers lost
between the clean and 75\% conditions, 13 become abstentions and 5 become answers
that are wrong but faithful to the corrupted context. None become fabrications. A
pooled analysis reports that failures rose from 54 to 72 and stops there; the
decomposition shows that the entire increase is absorbed by the two \emph{safe}
failure modes.

\paragraph{Our preregistered hypothesis was not supported.}
We predicted that silent failure --- the share of non-correct answers that are
assertions rather than abstentions --- would peak at partial corruption, on the
reasoning that a partly-plausible context licenses a confident guess where a wholly
useless one triggers refusal. The observed series is $0.185$, $0.203$, $0.219$,
$0.208$: an interior maximum, but with 95\% intervals of $[0.09, 0.30]$ and
$[0.12, 0.33]$ that overlap completely. A power analysis conducted before data
collection established that separating an interior peak at $n{=}150$ requires a
gap exceeding 16 points; the observed spread is 3. We report the hypothesis as
unsupported and underpowered rather than as a trend.

The construct itself proved less informative than its components. Because
\textsc{silent\_failure} pools unsupported assertions (flat) with grounded errors
(rising), the ratio is insensitive to the very decomposition that carries the
result. We recommend reporting the outcome counts directly.

\paragraph{Context size and position.}
Figure~\ref{fig:size} sweeps $K$ from 1 to 10 under clean retrieval. Answer F1 and
gold-in-context rate diverge as $K$ grows: retrieval keeps improving while answers
do not, which localises the loss in generation rather than retrieval. Holding $K=8$
and the context contents fixed while moving the gold chunk between the first,
middle and last slot tests \citet{liu2024lost} directly.

\paragraph{Predicting failure before generation.}
Table~\ref{tab:predict} gives the AUROC of each retrieval-side signal against two
targets: any non-correct answer, and the narrower case of an unsupported assertion.
The strongest predictor of an unsupported assertion is \NUM{bestSilentSignal} at
AUROC \NUM{bestSilentAUROC}.

The practical question is not the AUROC but whether a similarity threshold is worth
deploying, and that depends on how much worse a fabrication is than a refusal --- a
property of the application, not of the model. We therefore sweep that cost ratio
and report the break-even point: the factor by which a hallucination must outweigh a
correct answer before any threshold beats answering everything. Below an AUROC of
roughly 0.6 no threshold is viable at any cost ratio, and a system that needs to
suppress unsupported assertions must verify after generation rather than gate before
it. That is a useful result for practitioners either way, because the cheap gate is
the first thing most deployments reach for.

\paragraph{Does the synthetic perturbation reproduce real failure?}
The study's independent variable is artificial, so the results are only as good as
the manipulation's correspondence to retrieval that fails on its own. The clean
condition supplies the control: 53 items whose gold document was simply absent from
the top-$K$, with no intervention. Comparing those against synthetically
evidence-free items, the 95\% intervals overlap on every outcome --- correct,
abstain, unsupported and grounded error alike. In both cases the generator
overwhelmingly abstains (0.78 natural, 0.92 synthetic) and rarely asserts anything
unsupported (0.05 and 0.01).

We state this as the weak result it is: overlap at these sample sizes is evidence
against a large discrepancy, not evidence of equivalence. But it is the check a
reader is entitled to demand, and it costs nothing beyond data already collected.

\paragraph{Mitigations.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mitigation} plots each inference-time defence as a pair: change in
hallucination rate against change in correct rate, both paired against no
mitigation under the same degraded retrieval. Any instruction that pushes a model
toward abstention lowers hallucination; the question is whether it lowers it faster
than it discards correct answers. Only the upper-left quadrant is a win.

\section{Conclusion}
Across a range of retrieval corruption that costs \NUM{deltaCorrectPartial} points
of accuracy, the system studied here did not fabricate more; it refused more, and it
faithfully repeated more of the wrong evidence it was given. Only when the evidence
was removed outright did it recognise the problem, abstaining on
\NUM{abstainAtTotal}\% of questions --- which locates the practical risk not in
fabrication under degraded retrieval but in the generator's inability to tell
degraded evidence from good. Whether that reflects retrieval-augmented generation in
general or one instruction-tuned model's disposition toward abstention is the
obvious next question, and this design answers it by swapping a single argument.

\section*{Limitations}
% Required at *ACL venues. Reviewers reward candour here; the facts are
% discoverable either way.
Our judge is currently the same checkpoint as the generator
(\NUM{judgeModel} judging \NUM{genModel}), so faithfulness verdicts are
self-evaluation; every results file records this flag, and agreement against human
labels is reported in the repository rather than assumed. All findings concern a
single generator family and do not establish cross-model generality. Abstention is
detected by a lexical rule whose precision and recall are measured on a
hand-labelled subset but which will misclassify unusual phrasings. The corpus is
roughly 1.3k chunks --- small enough that claims should not be extrapolated to
production-scale knowledge bases. Conditions are run once at temperature 0; a
single deterministic sample is not the same as an estimate of sampling variance.
Finally, the perturbations are synthetic by construction: they isolate failure
modes cleanly at the cost of not being drawn from any observed deployment
distribution.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work uses public QA benchmarks and involves no human subjects and no personal
data. It characterises conditions under which retrieval-augmented systems assert
unsupported claims; the intent is to make such failures measurable and detectable,
and the mitigation analysis is reported with its accuracy cost so that it cannot be
read as a solved problem.

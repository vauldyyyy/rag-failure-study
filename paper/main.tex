\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is deployed on the premise that grounding
suppresses hallucination, and the corresponding fear is that degraded retrieval
reintroduces it. We measure the \emph{composition} of RAG failures under
per-item-exact corruption on \NUM{nQA} SQuAD~v2 questions, across nine conditions
spanning irrelevant, adjacent-but-wrong, falsified and removed evidence, and
replicate the full grid on a generator from a second model family. Unsupported
assertion rises in no condition for either generator after FDR correction (no 95\%
interval above \(\NUM{halluMaxUpper}\) and \(\NUM{halluMaxUpperTwo}\) points). What
changes is which failure absorbs the loss. When evidence is absent, generators
abstain. When it is falsified, accuracy falls by \NUM{deltaCorrectCF} and
\(\NUM{cfDeltaCorrectTwo}\) points, abstention does not significantly rise, and
answers that are wrong but faithful to the corrupted context rise by
\(+\)\NUM{deltaGroundedCF} and \(\NUM{cfDeltaGroundedTwo}\) points
(\(q < 0.0001\) for both). The generators distinguish \emph{no} evidence from
\emph{some} evidence, but not true evidence from false, so the defence of abstaining
under uncertainty never fires. An independent judge did not reproduce the primary
judge's \textsc{unsupported} labels; both findings survive discarding those labels. A
preregistered prediction that silent failure peaks at partial corruption was not
supported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RAG \citep{lewis2020rag} is the standard industrial answer to hallucination: retrieve
evidence, condition on it, and the model stops inventing. The premise holds only as
far as retrieval does. What matters when retrieval fails is not only \emph{how much}
accuracy falls but \emph{what the system does instead}. A system that says ``I cannot
answer from the context'' has failed safely; one that answers fluently and wrongly
has not, yet both register identically as an accuracy drop. RAG benchmarks
\citep{chen2024rgb,cuconasu2024noise} report accuracy under noise, and
\citet{chen2024rgb} score negative rejection as a capability, but neither traces how
the balance between abstention and assertion moves as retrieval degrades.

We find the fear of fabrication misplaced and the real risk elsewhere. Missing
evidence produces refusal; \emph{false} evidence produces a confident, grounded, wrong
answer with no sign that anything is amiss. That models adopt coherent false evidence
is not new \citep{xie2024chameleon,wu2024clasheval}. What that literature does not
measure is the rest of the outcome distribution: whether unsupported assertion moves
at the same time, and whether abstention responds to falsified evidence as it does to
absent evidence. Our contribution is that decomposition, within one matched design:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item unsupported assertion rises under none of nine corruption conditions, for
      either of two generators from different families (Tables~\ref{tab:noise},
      \ref{tab:xmodel});
\item failure mode is governed by the \emph{kind} of degradation: absent evidence
      yields abstention, while falsified evidence yields grounded error with no
      significant rise in abstention;
\item a taxonomy separating unsupported assertion from grounded error, without which
      neither result is observable, and a perturbation protocol that fixes each
      context's corruption exactly;
\item a direct test of how far these conclusions depend on the LLM judge.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\citet{chen2024rgb} evaluate noise robustness, negative rejection, information
integration and counterfactual robustness; our perturbations follow that taxonomy and
we claim no novelty for it. \citet{cuconasu2024noise} find that random documents can
raise accuracy while related ones depress it. Our counterfactual condition is a
context--memory conflict \citep{xu2024conflictsurvey} constructed following
\citet{longpre2021conflicts}. \citet{wu2024clasheval} find six models adopt incorrect
retrieved content over a correct prior more than 60\% of the time, and our accuracy
collapse replicates that direction; such studies measure \emph{which answer} is
adopted and do not compare abstention under falsified versus absent evidence.
\citet{zou2025poisonedrag} steer RAG with optimised malicious passages. Ours are not
optimised --- one answer string is replaced --- which suggests the failure needs no
adversary. \citet{liu2024lost} show accuracy depends on passage position; we fix
$K{=}4$ and do not vary position. Confidence-based abstention
\citep{kadavath2022know,asai2024selfrag} needs model access or extra generation; we
test retrieval scores, which are free. RAG evaluation frameworks
\citep{es2024ragas,saadfalcon2024ares} supply faithfulness metrics; our judge is
narrower, ruling only on the context actually shown.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Data and models.}
\NUM{nQA} questions from the SQuAD~v2 development set \citep{rajpurkar2018squad2}, seed
\NUM{randomSeed}: \NUM{nAnswerable} answerable and \NUM{nUnanswerable} unanswerable.
The corpus is every paragraph in the split, chunked at 256 tokens with 50-token
overlap, embedded with \NUM{embedModel} and searched exactly (FAISS
\texttt{IndexFlatIP}). The generators, at temperature 0, are \NUM{genModel} and the
open-weights \NUM{genModelTwo}. Perturbation conditions are judged by \NUM{judgeModel};
the primary run's clean baseline by \NUM{judgeModelBaseline}. No judge is a generator.

\paragraph{Perturbations.}
Corruption is applied to the context handed to the generator, not to the index, so the
fraction is exact per item and retrieval is byte-identical across conditions. We use
\textsc{random} (irrelevant chunks), \textsc{related} (lower-ranked real retrievals:
plausible, wrong), \textsc{counterfactual} (the gold answer string replaced by a false
one \citep{longpre2021conflicts}) and \textsc{drop\_gold} (gold chunks removed) at
fractions of a $K{=}4$ context.

\paragraph{Outcomes.}
Each generation receives one label: \textsc{abstain}; \textsc{correct} (token F1
$\geq 0.5$); \textsc{unsupported} (asserts what the context does not support);
\textsc{incorrect\_grounded} (wrong, but traceable to the context); or \textsc{invalid}
(no usable generation, excluded from rates). Only \textsc{unsupported} counts as
hallucination. Abstention is detected by rule, and a refusal followed by a claim counts
as an assertion. For answerable items neither abstained nor correct, a judge sees the
question, answer and the context shown and rules on support. Unanswerable items need no
judge: any assertion is unsupported by construction. Of \NUM{nScoredRows} scored
primary generations the judge was invoked on \NUM{judgeInvocationPct}\%, and
\NUM{ruleDerivedPct}\% of the \NUM{nUnsupported} unsupported labels are rule-derived.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Rates carry 95\% bootstrap intervals over questions. Comparisons are paired bootstraps
on the same questions; an invalid row drops the pair. Comparisons against clean form
one family per outcome, corrected by Benjamini--Hochberg \citep{benjamini1995fdr}.

\paragraph{Hallucination does not rise.}
Under related noise, correctness falls by $3.3$, $6.7$ and $12.0$ points at 25, 50 and
75\% ($p<0.0001$ at 75\%), while unsupported assertion differs from clean by at most
one item ($p \geq 0.72$; intervals within $\pm$\NUM{halluBoundPartial} points). Of 18
correct answers lost at 75\%, 13 become abstentions, 5 grounded errors, and none
fabrications. Across all nine conditions, eight are null after correction and the
ninth is a significant \emph{decrease} under total random corruption, where the
generator abstains almost always.

\paragraph{Falsified evidence is different.}
At 50\% counterfactual corruption grounded error rises from \NUM{groundedClean}\% to
\NUM{groundedCF}\%, roughly tenfold, and accuracy falls \NUM{deltaCorrectCF} points,
while abstention does not significantly rise ($p = 0.06$). At total related corruption,
by contrast, the generator abstains on \NUM{abstainAtTotal}\% of questions.

\paragraph{Silent failure: preregistered prediction not supported.}
We predicted that the share of non-correct answers that are assertions would peak at
partial corruption. The primary series ($0.185$, $0.203$, $0.219$, $0.208$) has an
interior maximum, but its intervals overlap completely, and the second generator's
series declines from 25\% corruption onward. We report the hypothesis as unsupported.

\paragraph{Retrieval signals and synthetic validity.}
The strongest retrieval-side predictor of an unsupported assertion, \NUM{bestSilentSignal},

\paragraph{Strict prompting misses the failure.}
Under counterfactual corruption, an instruction to answer only from the context and
otherwise refuse changes unsupported assertion by \(\NUM{mitDeltaHallu}\)
(\(p \NUM{mitPHallu}\)), abstention by \(\NUM{mitDeltaAbstain}\)
(\(p \NUM{mitPAbstain}\)), and correct answers by \(\NUM{mitDeltaCorrect}\)
(\(p \NUM{mitPCorrect}\)). Grounded error does not fall
(\(\NUM{mitDeltaGrounded}\), \(p \NUM{mitPGrounded}\)). A false but coherent passage
presents no signal of uncertainty to act on, so refusals are untargeted.

\section{Conclusion}
Across two model families, corrupting retrieval did not produce more fabrication. Absent
evidence produced refusal; falsified evidence produced faithful repetition without
refusal. Faithfulness is not truthfulness, and the risk lies in corrupted knowledge
bases rather than hallucination. Defences need to verify evidence, since
uncertainty-based abstention does not fire.

\section*{Limitations}
% Required at *ACL venues. Reviewers reward candour here; the facts are
% discoverable either way.
Two generators are not a population: both are instruction-tuned models served through
one provider, and the result may not extend to others. The primary judge shares a
family with the primary generator, and two judge checkpoints were used. Judge labels
have not been validated against human annotation, and an independent model judge did
not reproduce the primary judge's \textsc{unsupported} labels; the headline
conclusions are stated so as to hold under rule-derived labels alone. The second
generator lost \NUM{invalidPctTwo}\% of generations non-randomly; the complete-case
analysis addresses differential loss between conditions but not selection on which
questions are observed, so its absolute rates describe easier questions. Abstention
is detected by a lexical rule that will misclassify unusual phrasings. The corpus is
roughly 1.3k chunks and should not be extrapolated to production scale. Conditions
are run once at temperature 0, which this API does not make fully deterministic: an
independent re-run of the 50\% counterfactual condition gave
\NUM{mitCorrectNonePct}\% correct against \NUM{correctCF}\% in Table~\ref{tab:noise}.
Perturbations are synthetic and isolate failure modes at the cost of not being drawn
from any observed deployment distribution.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work uses public QA benchmarks and involves no human subjects and no personal
data. It characterises conditions under which retrieval-augmented systems assert
unsupported claims; the intent is to make such failures measurable and detectable,
and the mitigation analysis is reported with its accuracy cost so that it cannot be
read as a solved problem.
